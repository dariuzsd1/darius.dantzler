Europe's New Space sector doesn't lack ambition. It lacks a map. Funding mechanisms, incubator programs, and national space agency initiatives are scattered across a dozen countries and twice as many acronyms, and most founders learn the terrain the expensive way: by getting rejected from a program they didn't fully understand in the first place.

SpaceHub started as an answer to a simple question: what does it actually take to get a New Space company from idea to funded in Europe? Not the pitch-deck version. The real one, with the paperwork, the eligibility rules, and the difference between a program that writes you a check and one that just wants a logo for its website.

**What the research covers**

- **ESA Business Incubation Centres (BIC):** how the network of national BICs differs in funding size, equity terms, and in-kind support, and which ones actually move fast
- **Accelerators:** the landscape of space-focused accelerators operating across Europe, what stage they target, and what they expect in return
- **Public funding mechanisms:** national and EU-level grant programs, how they stack with private funding, and the sequencing that avoids leaving money on the table

**Why it matters**

Space security and sustainability work doesn't happen without a healthy commercial ecosystem underneath it. Policy that's disconnected from how New Space companies actually get built and funded ends up solving problems that don't exist and missing the ones that do. SpaceHub is the groundwork for policy recommendations that hold up against how the industry actually operates, not how it's assumed to.

This is an active, ongoing project. The map gets more detailed every month.
