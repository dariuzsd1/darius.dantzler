Export control is the part of the space industry nobody wants to think about until it stops a deal cold. ITAR and EAR classification determines what technology can be shared, with whom, and under what conditions, and getting it wrong doesn't just slow a company down. It can end it, or land it in front of a regulator with a much worse set of questions.

Team VERA was an ISU sprint project built around a direct bet: export compliance checking is a process problem before it's a legal problem, and process problems are exactly what AI tooling is good at narrowing down, if not fully solving.

**What we built**

Over the course of the sprint, the team took VERA from concept to a working, presentable product:

- **MVP and website**: a functioning prototype demonstrating AI-assisted classification support for ITAR/EAR compliance questions, paired with a public-facing site explaining the product to a non-technical audience
- **Company video**: a short film pitching the problem and the product to investors and industry partners
- **Product-market fit report**: research into who actually needs this, how they handle compliance today, and where the willingness to pay lives
- **Live pitch**: presented the concept and findings to a room of ISU faculty, industry mentors, and peers

**My role**

I worked the compliance and market side of the project: translating ITAR/EAR requirements into something a product team could actually build against, and stress-testing the business case against how compliance teams really operate day to day. Export control is a discipline built on precedent and caution. The product had to earn trust with compliance officers, not just impress a pitch panel.

**Why it matters**

Space security work is, at its core, about making sure the right technology moves and the wrong technology doesn't. VERA is that principle built into software.
